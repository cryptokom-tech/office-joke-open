# Candidates — local judging for the Office Joke Open

Judge brief (in order): (1) about office life, works as a joke; (2) the laugh
is earned — a turn, misdirection or recognition that pays off in the last few
words; (3) a specific office detail, not a generic grumble; (4) fresh — a
familiar setup taken somewhere unexpected; (5) tight, reads cleanly in one
pass. All 16 candidates are original, written in this session; no entry text
was copied from the field (the public API shows submission titles only).

## Pairwise verdicts (first differing criterion decides)

- **I vs K:** I wins on the turn (2) — a lunch outlasting its owner is a bigger
  surprise than a committee escalating to a task force — and on freshness (4).
- **I vs C:** I wins on (2) — a three-beat story arc beats a single twist —
  and on (3): fridge, Dave, 2021 is more concrete than a calendar.
- **I vs H:** I wins on (4). The trust fall is a known setup; Dave's lunch is
  somewhere the reader has not been. Close.
- **I vs P:** I wins on (2) — three beats against one literalisation of
  "circle back".
- **I vs F:** I wins on (2) — "longest-serving employee" lands harder than
  "put it in the roadmap", and "survive until Friday" is a familiar line.
- **I vs E:** I wins on (4) — printer jokes are the most common office gag in
  the field; the fridge lunch is not.
- **I vs A / B:** I wins on (4) — reply-all storms and meetings-about-meetings
  are familiar setups; I takes a familiar object (a labelled lunch) somewhere
  unexpected.
- **K vs C:** K wins on (2)/(3) — intern vs committee is a richer scene than
  the self-inviting calendar.
- **C vs F:** C wins on (4) — a calendar inviting itself is fresher than a
  five-year plan.
- **H vs F:** H wins on (2) — the three-beat fall with the ironic closer.
- **G, J, L, M, N, O, D:** all pass (1) and (3), but their payoffs are flatter
  or subtler — G is irony without a laugh, J's "synced nothing" is the expected
  punch, M/N/O/D land as remarks as much as jokes.

## Ranking

1. **I — Dave's lunch (submitted).** Three beats, specific detail, the twist
   in the last words, fresh.
2. K — the intern and the task force. Strong satire, slightly more familiar.
3. H — the trust fall. Best three-beat structure after I, known setup.
4. C — the self-inviting calendar. Freshest single twist after I.
5. P — the roundabout. Tightest line, one note.
6. F — survive until Friday. Good turn, familiar opening line.
